# Proof status

Last reviewed: 2026-08-01, after consolidation from `rabsef-talwet`, temporary `cgol` CI branches, and retained local audit material.

## Current best defensible statement

The repository contains a substantial proof program for the regular-octagon honeycomb problem. The crystalline-direction theorem has strong formal support, including kernel-checked analytic and finite-combinatorial components. The arbitrary-direction extension has kernel-checked phase geometry and classification components plus a separately implemented Boost/MPFR local interval certificate.

The unrestricted regular-octagon honeycomb conjecture is **not yet claimed proved**. No present theorem starts from an actual normal convex tiling of the plane and derives the final average-perimeter inequality without additional abstract payment or modeling hypotheses.

## Established or strongly certified components

- Exact RR-edge coordinate algebra and the crystalline pair-gap analysis.
- Finite crystalline selected-edge counting and debt-payment modules.
- A sharp conditioned-edge inequality for an arbitrary fixed normal fan.
- Wedge-splitting algebra and analytic phase-defect bounds across all angular sectors.
- Dangerous-turn classification and normalized owner root-box selection.
- A twelve-case local noncrystalline interval calculation using directed-rounding Boost, independently replayed with 192-bit MPFR arithmetic.
- A generated Lean import checking the finite certificate partition and exact positive leaf metadata.
- Side and corner charge banks, ordinary role capacities, collision grouping, and collision-credit estimates in finite abstract models.
- Explicit provenance and archived failed approaches.

## Current open obligations

1. **Unified formal graph.** Reconcile the canonical integrated snapshot and the later standalone modules into one maintained Lean project, resolving name duplication and superseded variants.
2. **Actual tiling model.** Define the finite geometric tiling object used by the unrestricted proof, including cells, physical edges, trivalent and nontrivalent vertices, subdivisions, and boundary data.
3. **Selected-edge construction.** Derive the ordinary/collision owner partition and every role map from that tiling object rather than accepting abstract maps and multiplicity hypotheses.
4. **Local evaluator bridge.** Prove that the geometric data at every ordinary selected edge satisfy the exact six-variable formula and domain used by the external interval certificate.
5. **Global finite closure.** Combine local ordinary payments, collision payments, positive-cell banks, Euler or high-valence credits, and boundary terms in one theorem.
6. **Plane exhaustion.** Pass from finite patches or a toroidal model to a normal convex tiling of the plane with an explicit vanishing-boundary estimate.
7. **Independent audit and novelty review.** Compare the final statement and proof mechanism against current literature before any public breakthrough claim.

## Next proof target

The highest-leverage next theorem is the **geometric local-evaluator bridge**: given an actual selected edge in the finite tiling model, construct the owner and neighbor fan parameters, prove they lie in the certified domain, and identify the certified scalar expression with an account-safe lower bound for that owner's net charge.

## Claim discipline

The repository deliberately retains failed certificates and prior overclaims. A green neighboring module is not evidence that an unstated interface theorem has been proved. `TRUST_BOUNDARY.md` and this file control the project’s public claims.
